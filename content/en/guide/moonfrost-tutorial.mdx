## Getting Started: Core Mechanics and Day-One Priorities

When you wake up on your farm for the first time, your initial instinct might be to clear every rock and tree branch in sight. Doing so, however, will drain your energy meter before midday arrives, leaving your avatar exhausted and forced into an early bedtime. In this opening phase of our **Moonfrost tutorial**, the golden rule is deliberate pacing.

Every physical action—swinging an axe, tiling garden soil, or hauling water—costs stamina. Time continuously moves forward, and passing out from exhaustion incurs severe penalties, including reduced starting stamina on the following day. To establish early momentum, your very first day should follow a rigid schedule focused on clearing a tight gardening perimeter, planting your initial starter seeds, and introducing yourself to key townsfolk.

### The Day-One Action Plan

Do not attempt to clear the entire property immediately. Instead, carve out an 8x8 plot directly in front of your cabin door. This minimizes walking distance during early mornings when every second of stamina conservation counts.

| Task | Energy Cost | Priority Level | Recommended Time of Day | Primary Benefit |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Clear 12 Garden Tiles | ~24 Stamina | High | 06:00 – 08:00 | Prepares fertile ground for starter crops |
| Till and Water Starter Seeds | ~20 Stamina | High | 08:00 – 10:00 | Starts the growth timer immediately |
| Explore Main Town Square | 0 Stamina | Medium | 10:00 – 14:00 | Meets key vendors and initiates quests |
| Forage Forest Perimeter | ~15 Stamina | Medium | 14:00 – 18:00 | Collects free wild edibles for energy restoration |
| Early Crafting & Storage | ~10 Stamina | High | 18:00 – 21:00 | Empties bag space before sleep |

Player experience suggests keeping at least 15% of your stamina bar intact before heading indoors at dusk. Random night events or sudden crafting needs can arise, and a small energy cushion prevents accidental late-night collapses.

Your starter toolset provides the bare minimum required to interact with your surroundings. As highlighted throughout this **Moonfrost tutorial**, prioritizing your tool bench and collecting basic raw materials—such as raw timber, stone chunks, and fiber—dictates how fast your farm expands.

Upgrading your gear reduces the stamina tax per swing and widens your area of effect. For example, moving from a makeshift watering can to a reinforced copper-grade can allows you to water three tiles simultaneously instead of one, cutting your morning chore time by more than half.

## Agriculture and Foraging: Maximizing Your Daily Yield

Farming is the financial engine that funds your building upgrades, animal pens, and decorative expansions. A key lesson in any thorough **Moonfrost tutorial** is understanding crop turnaround times, profit margins, and daily watering schedules.

Crops must be watered every day unless rain rolls across the valley. Skipping a single watering day pauses plant development, delaying your harvest and potentially stranding your crops between seasonal shifts. If an unharvested crop remains in the ground when the season changes, it will instantly wither into dead mulch.

## Advanced Homestead Strategy: Organization and Seasonal Shifts

As you move past your initial two weeks, your farm layout requires structured design to avoid wasted travel time. Chaotic field arrangements lead to wasted movements, and in a life-sim where stamina and minutes are tied directly to productivity, efficiency reigns supreme.

### Spatial Planning and Farm Zones

Organize your homestead into distinct operational hubs:

*   **The Agricultural Core:** Kept immediately adjacent to your cabin and water source. Arrange crops in 3x3 grids around future sprinkler locations.
*   **The Production Yard:** Group your furnaces, charcoal kilns, and timber mills in a single paved area. Pavement increases your character's running speed compared to bare soil or grass.
*   **The Pasture Zone:** Reserve open, grassy land further from the house for animal coops and barns. Animals need room to graze outside on sunny days, which saves you the expense of buying animal feed.
*   **The Tree Orchard:** Plant fruit saplings along the outer edges of your property with two empty tiles of clearance in every direction so their growth remains unobstructed.

Preparing for end-of-season transitions prevents catastrophic crop losses. On day 25 of any 28-day season, stop planting slow-growth crops. Shift your labor toward hoarding seasonal forageables, chopping excess wood, clearing deep mining floors, and upgrading your tools while your agricultural fields sit dormant.

Wrapping up this **Moonfrost tutorial** with long-term layout strategies ensures that when seasonal blizzards or intense summer heatwaves roll across the countryside, your homestead remains fully functional and lucrative.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Moonfrost

### How long does it take to complete the basic Moonfrost tutorial quests?
The initial introductory quests typically take between three to five in-game days to complete. This gives you sufficient time to learn basic mechanics, meet the villagers, clear an initial crop field, and craft your first storage units without rushing.

### What happens if my character passes out from exhaustion?
If your stamina reaches zero or you stay out past 02:00 AM, your character collapses. You wake up the next morning in your bed with only 50% of your maximum stamina restored, and a local doctor or villager may charge a small fee for carrying you home safely.

### Can I change my farm layout or move buildings later?
Yes. Buildings, chests, fences, and artisan machines can be relocated. While moving crops destroys active seed plantings, physical structures and decorative tiles can be rearranged at any time without losing the original crafting materials.

### Does rain affect my farm chores and fishing chances?
Rain automatically waters every tilled tile on your homestead, saving you an entire morning of stamina expenditure. Furthermore, rainy weather causes rare, high-value fish to emerge in local rivers and the ocean, making wet weather ideal for prolonged angling expeditions.
